# Honest project writing

Read PROJECT-FACTS.md and the project brief. Use a calm first-person voice where personal ownership is known. Prefer concrete verbs and short explanations to promotional claims.

1. Separate supplied facts, repository-verified implementation, personal contribution, learning goals and unknown details. Project features alone do not prove the owner's individual contribution.
2. Inspect source and configuration where available before naming exact implementations. Preserve supplied URLs; omit missing links. Keep provenance in a private content evidence file.
3. Never invent users, metrics, dates, seniority, credentials, production status, demos, screenshots, accuracy, pricing or engineering outcomes. An unavailable repo is an access limitation, not evidence of absence.
4. Explicitly label VisionPro and sqly as in progress. Treat sqly's B-tree/pages/cursor/persistence ideas as roadmap until verified; do not claim transactions, recovery or ACID support without evidence.
5. Describe VisionPro as comparing served goods with cashier records and summarizing daily totals. List RTSP cameras, mini PC/Frigate, MQTT, SQLite and a possible web dashboard as technical direction, without asserting completed integration. Do not infer theft detection, facial recognition, tracking or deployed model capabilities.
6. Describe Stride's supplied learning features and subscription/free-trial model. Do not imply official SHRM/HRCI affiliation or treat PHR as a separate certification body. Verify delivery status.
7. For each case study, explain the problem, workflow, verified contribution, implementation decisions, tradeoffs and current state. Keep unknown sections short rather than fabricating depth.
8. Keep missing details in CONTENT-NEEDED.md. Render only supported public statements. No placeholder contact buttons or fabricated blog posts; keep drafts excluded from publication and feeds.

Final audit: every factual claim is attributable, unfinished work stays labeled, and straightforward copy remains confident without inflation or self-deprecation.
